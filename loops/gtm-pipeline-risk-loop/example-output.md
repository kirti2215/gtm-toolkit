**Blackwell Capital — Priority 1**

- **Risk type:** Stakeholder risk + Process / timing risk
  A new VP of Technology entered the deal last week. At $520K — approximately 3x
  James's average deal size — this person will almost certainly want to re-examine
  the vendor decision before signing. No executive relationship exists with this new
  stakeholder. Legal has not been engaged on a deal with an August 25 close date,
  which is now 3 weeks away.

- **Why this matters:** This is the highest-value deal in the pipeline. If the new
  VP resets the evaluation, the close date moves to Q4 at minimum. Combined with
  James covering two territories, this deal is at risk of receiving insufficient
  attention at exactly the wrong moment.

- **What the rep should do in the next 48 hours:** James needs to request an
  introduction to the new VP of Technology this week — framed not as a sales call
  but as an onboarding briefing: "We want to make sure you have everything you need
  to feel confident in this decision." This meeting needs to happen before August 10
  or the deal will almost certainly push.

- **What success looks like:** A confirmed meeting with the new VP scheduled before
  August 10, and confirmation that legal review will begin this week.

- **Escalation trigger:** If James cannot get the VP introduction scheduled by
  August 7, the Loop recommends manager escalation to an executive-to-executive
  outreach — this deal is too large to let slip on an access problem. Final decision
  on escalation rests with the sales manager.

- **Intelligence gap:** We do not know the new VP's technical orientation or whether
  they have an existing vendor preference. That shapes how the introduction meeting
  should be framed and is the most important missing context in the current pipeline.

**Northgate Health — Priority 4**

  Security review has not started on a deal with a September 5 close date. Healthcare
  organizations with HIPAA requirements typically run security reviews over multiple
  weeks — the exact timeline varies by organization, but with 7 weeks to close,
  starting this week versus next week could determine whether this deal closes on
  time or slips to next quarter.

- **What the rep should do in the next 48 hours:** Sarah needs to ask the champion
  to initiate the security review process today. Frame it as protecting their
  timeline: "To hit your September 5 target, security review needs to start this
  week. Can you introduce me to your security team or share their standard vendor
  questionnaire so we can get ahead of it?"

- **What success looks like:** Security review initiated before August 7.

- **Escalation trigger:** If security review is not initiated by August 10, the Loop
  recommends adjusting the close date to October. That timeline change is the sales
  manager's decision.

**Cascade Logistics — Priority 5**

- **Risk type:** Competitive risk (motion unconfirmed)
  DataStax is in this deal, introduced by the customer's data engineering team who
  prefer its query flexibility. James has not run competitive positioning yet. Before
  recommending a specific competitive response, the Loop needs to understand the
  motion: (1) head-to-head feature evaluation — where a competitive battle card is
  the right tool; (2) developer preference / identity play — where the engineering
  team has already formed a preference and the decision is not primarily about
  features. These require different interventions.

- **What the rep should do in the next 48 hours:** Run a discovery conversation
  before any competitive materials are prepared. Key questions: Has DataStax been
  used in this environment before? Is this an evaluation or a preference ratification?
  Who has final vendor authority — the engineering team or a business or procurement
  stakeholder? The answers determine which play to run.

- **Do not generate a battle card yet.** A feature comparison battle card is the
  right artifact for a head-to-head evaluation. It is the wrong artifact if the
  engineering team's preference is already formed — in that case, the better
  intervention is opening a parallel thread with a business-side stakeholder.
  Using the wrong artifact in the wrong motion risks reinforcing a framing that
  doesn't help the deal.

- **What success looks like:** A follow-up call confirming which competitive motion
  is active, so the right intervention can be designed.

- **Skill invocation flag (conditional):** Generate competitive battle card after
  discovery confirms a feature evaluation motion. Competitor = DataStax, vertical =
  logistics/supply chain, buyer = data engineering team.
