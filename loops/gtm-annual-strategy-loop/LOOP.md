# Annual GTM Strategy and Goal Governance Loop

**Version:** 2.0

---

## What This Loop Is

The Annual GTM Strategy and Goal Governance Loop governs a portfolio of strategic
bets against a set of annual commitments from initial portfolio formation through
year-end learning.

It operates in two distinct capacities across the fiscal year.

**During Planning mode**, the Loop forms and pressure-tests the annual portfolio.
It synthesizes leadership intent, historical internal evidence, external market
evidence, capacity constraints, dependencies, and prior-year learning into a coherent
set of commitments, bets, assumptions, seasonal calendars, and contingencies. The
Loop may help construct the plan. It may not invent unsupported strategy from no
evidence, and it does not substitute for leadership judgment about direction.

A GTM Strategy Formation Skill may be invoked inside Planning mode to accelerate
evidence synthesis, segment analysis, or scenario modeling. This remains one
stateful Loop. The portfolio that Planning mode produces becomes the state the
Loop governs through the rest of the year.

**During Monitoring, Adaptation, and Endgame and Learning**, the Loop tracks
evidence against that portfolio, infers state across all dimensions, generates
diagnoses, recommends decisions, and evaluates whether deployed interventions
produced the intended change.

The Loop is not a planning document. It is a planning system that runs continuously.

---

## The Problem It Solves

A static annual plan has a specific and well-known failure mode: it is set in January
with a set of assumptions, those assumptions begin to break in March, and the
organization does not formally recognize the failure until the Q3 QBR. By then, the
seasonal window has closed, the replacement-bet options have narrowed, and the only
remaining action is explanation — not intervention.

The Loop addresses this failure at the source by tracking the assumptions that made
the plan credible in the first place. When those assumptions weaken or fail, the
Loop identifies which bets are affected, infers the appropriate operating mode, and
generates mode-specific recommendations — before the seasonal window closes and
before the organization loses the ability to act.

A second failure mode is equally common: organizations that revise their plan values
to restore apparent credibility rather than to record what they have actually learned.
An assumption revised downward to match a struggling indicator is not new insight.
It is a plan repair masquerading as an update. The Loop preserves what the
organization originally believed, tracks what caused it to change, and permanently
distinguishes new learning from target accommodation.

A third failure mode is the one that makes the first two recur: the organization
deploys interventions but does not rigorously evaluate whether they worked. The Loop
closes this gap. Every recommendation it makes is followed by an observation window,
a stated expected signal, and a structured evaluation when the window closes. The
Loop learns from its own recommendations, not just from external evidence.

## Core Principles

**1. The user supplies evidence. The Loop infers analytical state.**
The user reports facts, evidence, and organizational context. The Loop determines
operating mode, assumption status, bet health, root cause, seasonal position,
readiness, dependency confidence, and recommendations. No status judgment appears
in the user's input.

**2. Mode is inferred, not declared.**
The user supplies a review context — annual planning, monthly operating review, QBR,
urgent reassessment, executive decision review, year-end review. The Loop infers
which operating mode applies from timing, evidence, thresholds, and organizational
events. A declared review context informs but does not determine operating mode.

**3. Commitments are independent; bets may affect multiple commitments.**
An annual plan typically carries several simultaneous commitments — New ARR,
retention, pipeline coverage, partner contribution, gross margin. A bet may
contribute positively to one and negatively to another. Every recommendation must
state its effect on every commitment it touches, not only the primary one.

**4. Assumptions have two independent status tracks.**
Every assumption carries a structural status (is the underlying premise still valid?)
and a current-horizon status (can this assumption still generate contribution within
the current fiscal year?). These tracks are independent and may diverge.

**5. Seasonality is a first-class input.**
The best season is won before it begins. The Loop tracks seasonal position and
readiness ahead of contribution shortfalls. A bet entering its peak window without
completing its pipeline-build window has already missed its primary opportunity,
regardless of how much activity executes during peak.

**6. Plan versions are immutable.**
The Loop may revise the plan. It must never rewrite what the organization originally
believed. Every revision is recorded in the Version Ledger alongside the original
value. Prior assessments calculated under old values remain visible.

**7. Owner-stated confidence is not evidence.**
A bet owner who says their assumption is High confidence has supplied a judgment,
not a fact. The Loop separately infers Evidence-Assessed Confidence from the quality,
quantity, recency, and type of evidence supplied. Absent supporting evidence, the
result is Insufficient Evidence — not Low confidence. Low confidence means available
evidence actively provides weak support or meaningful contradiction. Missing evidence
and negative evidence are not the same.

**8. Hold is always Option 0.**
The Loop evaluates continuing the current course before evaluating alternatives.
Hold has explicit costs and benefits. The Loop never presents intervention as the
default and hold as the alternative.

**9. Guardrails apply to every recommendation.**
Every proposed intervention is checked against the guardrail metrics defined for
the affected bets before the Loop surfaces it. A recommendation that improves primary
commitment contribution while breaching a guardrail is a tradeoff, not an improvement.
The Loop surfaces the tradeoff rather than hiding it.

**10. The Loop closes the learning loop.**
Every intervention the Loop recommends receives a baseline, an expected leading
signal, a signal date, an observation window, and a structured evaluation when the
window closes. The Loop assesses whether each intervention worked and what to do next.
Recommendations the organization never learns from are not recommendations — they
are gestures.

## Operating Modes

The Loop operates in exactly four modes. Portfolio mode and bet-level mode are
independent. Multiple bets may be in different modes simultaneously.

### Planning

The Loop forms and pressure-tests the annual portfolio. Using leadership intent,
historical performance, external evidence, capacity data, and prior-year learning,
the Loop evaluates the quality of each bet hypothesis, the strength of supporting
evidence, the realism of seasonal calendars, the closure of the contribution bridge,
and the confidence level of dependencies. It surfaces gaps, challenges unsupported
assumptions, and flags risk concentrations before they are locked into the plan.

Planning mode ends when the portfolio is formally approved and initialized in the
Loop with all required fields.

### Monitoring

The portfolio is active and the Loop tracks signals against plan. Actuals are compared
against the seasonally expected path. Assumption indicators are read against defined
thresholds. Readiness gates are tracked. Dependencies are monitored. The Loop reports
current state across all six state dimensions, all seven health dimensions, and all
active commitments — and notes divergence from plan without triggering an intervention
recommendation unless a threshold condition is crossed.

Monitoring is the default mode for the majority of the fiscal year.

### Adaptation

A threshold has been crossed that warrants a portfolio or bet-level decision. The
Loop generates a root-cause diagnosis, applies the decision vocabulary, evaluates
options against all commitments and guardrails, and recommends a decision with a
named observation window.

Adaptation is triggered by failure signals and by upside signals. The Loop is not
only a failure-management system. Adaptation for an overperforming bet that could
absorb incremental investment is as legitimate as Adaptation for a failing one —
and more urgent when its seasonal window is closing.

Portfolio Adaptation is triggered only when a portfolio-level decision is required:
contribution replacement across bets, reallocation of shared resources, shared
dependency resolution, commitment revision, or portfolio-level conflict between
commitments. Single-bet interventions that do not require reallocation from other
bets do not require Portfolio Adaptation.

**Portfolio mode is driven by the state of ALL commitments, ALL guardrails, and
ALL shared dependencies — not only the primary commitment.** A portfolio may be
Monitoring on its primary revenue commitment while simultaneously requiring
Adaptation because a secondary NRR commitment is structurally at risk, a shared
delivery capacity guardrail has been breached, or a shared dependency failure
cascades across multiple bets. The portfolio's operating mode reflects its
most critical active condition across all of these dimensions.

Adaptation concludes when a decision is made, an intervention is deployed with a
defined observation window, and the Loop transitions back to Monitoring for that
window.

### Endgame and Learning

No new intervention can materially change the current-year result for one or more
bets. The Loop's function shifts from optimization to structured triage and capture.

This mode has two concurrent sections that shift in emphasis as the fiscal year closes:

**Current-year triage:** What contribution can still be recognized this fiscal year?
Which pipeline can realistically close? Which bets need to be carried forward rather
than pushed? Which commitments will be met, missed, or exceeded? What is the most
accurate forecast for the remainder of the year?

**Carry-forward learning:** What did the organization learn from each bet — about
the mechanism, the assumptions, the market, the buyers, the seasonal pattern, the
dependencies, and the interventions? Which bets should be continued, revised, or
retired? What should be initialized into next year's plan?

Fiscal close shifts the emphasis from triage to learning. It does not create a
separate operating mode.

Endgame and Learning is not failure. A bet that has reached its target and entered
the Endgame window is a successful bet in Endgame. A bet that is below target with
no remaining intervention window is in Endgame regardless of whether leadership
has formally acknowledged this.

## Mode Inference Rules

### Planning → Monitoring

Portfolio formally approved AND initialized in the Loop with all required fields.

### Any Mode → Adaptation

**Failure-side triggers:**
Adaptation is triggered when any of the following is confirmed by evidence:
- An Invalidated load-bearing assumption (structural or current-horizon)
- A Warning on a load-bearing assumption where the latest-useful-decision date is
  within the current observation window — there is no time to wait for the Warning
  to resolve before intervention loses its window
- A Warning pattern across several related assumptions whose combined portfolio impact
  is material
- Actuals materially below the seasonally expected path after a sufficient observation
  window has elapsed
- A required dependency has definitively failed with no recovery path
- A Required readiness gate assessed as Missed Unrecoverable
- A cross-bet conflict that requires a portfolio-level resource or priority decision

**Upside-side triggers:**
- A bet materially outperforming seasonal expectations in a way that suggests
  additional investment would generate incremental contribution
- A market event or competitor exit has opened an unplanned opportunity that
  requires near-term commitment
- A channel or partner is substantially outperforming its expected contribution
  in a way that warrants reallocation from underperforming bets

**Warning alone does not trigger Adaptation.** A Warning on a load-bearing
assumption produces:
- Assumption status moves to Warning
- Monitoring frequency increases
- A named decision deadline is set: the latest date at which a decision must be
  made even if no further evidence arrives
- Evidence acquisition steps are identified

Adaptation is triggered when Warning combines with either a compressed latest-useful
window, or a pattern of related Warnings with material combined impact.

### Any Mode → Endgame and Learning

The Endgame timing calculation is performed independently per bet and, during
Adaptation, per pivot option:

Remaining fiscal days − implementation lead time − field or partner ramp −
pipeline or demand build time − qualification / sales / adoption cycle −
procurement or operational realization − recognition lag ≤ 0

The bottleneck element is the longest stage that cannot be parallelized with others
for the specific intervention being evaluated. Different pivot options have different
timing chains and therefore different Endgame thresholds.

### Adaptation → Monitoring

Decision made, intervention deployed, observation window defined. The Loop
transitions back to Monitoring for the named window duration. Intervention
Evaluation begins.

## State Model

Each bet carries six independent state dimensions. The Loop updates all dimensions
on every run. State describes what is happening. Health (see next section) describes
how well it is going.

### Dimension 1 — Operating Mode

Planning / Monitoring / Adaptation / Endgame and Learning

Inferred from evidence, timing, and thresholds. Never declared by the user.

### Dimension 2 — Bet Maturity

Bet Maturity describes the evidence maturity of the bet's core logic — how well
supported the bet's hypothesis is by accumulated evidence. It does not describe
what is operationally happening.

**Planning basis:** The bet has been initialized with a hypothesis and planning-time
evidence. No live indicators have been read against plan.

**Signal immature:** Indicators have been instrumented and early readings exist,
but the signal window has not elapsed. Evidence is insufficient to confirm or
challenge the bet logic.

**Signal mature:** The signal window has elapsed and meaningful readings are
available, but the evidence does not yet confirm or contradict the core bet logic.
The bet is observable but not yet assessable.

**Bet logic supported:** Available evidence supports the core bet logic with at
least Medium evidence-assessed confidence. The Loop can rely on this bet's mechanism
for planning and reallocation decisions. Note: if the mechanism evidence is imported
from the Win/Loss Loop rather than independently established, this confidence
inherits the Win/Loss findings' limitations.

**Repeatedly supported:** Multiple independent evidence cycles have confirmed the
bet logic. The contribution path is reliably understood and the Loop can use this
bet as a stable planning anchor.

**Evidence contradicted:** Available evidence challenges or refutes the core bet
logic. The mechanism that was expected to generate contribution is under active
evidential challenge.

### Dimension 3 — Bet Operational Status

**Active:** The bet is executing normally against plan.

**Escalated:** A formal escalation brief has been issued. Leadership is aware of
a specific risk or decision point.

**Formally at risk:** Evidence has crossed a threshold requiring formal acknowledgment
that the bet may not meet its contribution target.

**Intervention deployed:** A specific intervention is active and in its defined
observation window. The Loop is tracking the leading signal against its expected
value. An Intervention Evaluation record has been opened.

**Discontinued:** The bet has been formally closed. Resources have been released
or redirected. The bet record is preserved.

**Carry-forward:** The bet's mechanism remains valid but contribution will not
close in the current fiscal year. The bet is positioned for the following year's
plan.

### Dimension 4 — Assumption Status

Every assumption carries two independent tracks. See the Assumption Framework.

### Dimension 5 — Season Position

Pre-season / Build / Pre-peak / Peak / Post-peak / Off-season

Inferred from the bet's seasonal calendar and the current date. Never stated by
the user.

### Dimension 6 — Season Readiness

Not started / In progress — on track / In progress — at risk /
Ready / Missed — recoverable / Missed — unrecoverable

Inferred from readiness gate evidence and the current date relative to the peak
window. Never stated by the user.

Ready means the bet is eligible for accelerated investment during peak. It does
not mean unlimited investment. The replacement-bet feasibility test still applies
if reallocation from another bet is being considered.

## Bet Health Model

The six state dimensions describe what is happening for each bet. The seven health
dimensions diagnose how well it is going and why divergence is occurring. State and
health are maintained separately and reported together.

**Health states for each dimension:**
Healthy / At risk / Degraded / Failed / Insufficient data

### The Central Diagnostic Question

Before any root-cause classification or decision recommendation is generated, the
Loop answers:

*Is this bet's performance diverging from plan because the organization has not yet
executed the intended strategy — in which case the response is execution-oriented —
or because executing the intended strategy will no longer produce the expected result
— in which case the response is strategic?*

The Execution/Capacity/Dependency health cluster answers the first question.
The Assumption/Contribution health cluster answers the second.

### Health Dimension 1 — Contribution Health

Are actuals tracking against the seasonally-expected contribution path? What is
the direction and magnitude of the gap? Is the gap widening, stable, or closing?
The Loop calculates the expected cumulative contribution to date based on the
seasonal curve and compares it to reported actuals.

### Health Dimension 2 — Assumption Health

Are the load-bearing assumptions behind this bet structurally intact and still
current-horizon active? Are indicator readings within normal variance? Have Warning
or Invalidation thresholds been crossed? The Loop synthesizes assumption status
across all of the bet's assumptions and produces an overall assumption health level,
noting which specific assumptions are driving that level.

### Health Dimension 3 — Execution Health

Is the intended strategy actually being executed at the required pace, completeness,
and quality? Are the planned activities happening? Are the expected outputs being
produced? Execution health separates "we are doing the wrong thing" from "we are
not doing the right thing." Evidence: activity reports, pipeline-stage progression,
field coverage, partner engagement, campaign performance.

### Health Dimension 4 — Capacity Health

Are the budget, headcount, partner capacity, and operational resources required to
execute the bet available and allocated? A bet can have healthy assumptions and poor
execution because the capacity to execute was never in place or was reallocated to
another priority. Evidence: budget actuals vs. plan, headcount vs. required coverage,
partner onboarding status, operational throughput.

### Health Dimension 5 — Dependency Health

Are the internal and external dependencies the bet requires on track? Have committed
dates been met? Is the confidence level of pending dependencies maintained? A bet
with healthy assumptions and capable execution can still fail if a required product
ship, partner integration, or regulatory approval does not arrive. Evidence: delivery
status of each dependency, any schedule changes, fallback plans.

### Health Dimension 6 — Intervention Health

If an intervention has been deployed for this bet, what does the Intervention
Evaluation record say? Is the leading signal present? Is it moving in the expected
direction and magnitude? Is the observation window still open, or has a result been
assessed? If no intervention is currently active, this dimension is N/A.

### Health Dimension 7 — Evidence Sufficiency

Is there enough evidence of the right type, recency, and quality to make a reliable
diagnosis of this bet? Are indicators instrumented? Is data quality sufficient to
distinguish signal from noise? Are there gaps that prevent the Loop from assessing
key dimensions? Evidence sufficiency is a prerequisite for reliable health assessment.
Low sufficiency elevates uncertainty in all other health dimensions.

## Root-Cause Diagnosis

Before generating any decision recommendation, the Loop classifies the divergence.
Root cause determines the appropriate decision type. A contribution gap caused by
an execution failure requires a different response than one caused by an assumption
failure — and applying the wrong response worsens the situation.

For each diagnosis, the Loop states:
- The root cause classification
- The primary evidence supporting this classification
- Competing explanations that have not been ruled out
- Unresolved uncertainty and the evidence that would resolve it
- The decision types that this root cause implies

### Classification 1 — Timing Lag

The strategy is working but contribution is arriving later than planned. The pipeline
is building, conversion is occurring, but on a different timetable than the seasonal
curve assumed. A timing lag may look identical to an execution gap in early data.
The Loop looks for signals that the mechanism is intact but slower: pipeline building
below pace but still positive, deals in later stages of the expected cycle, indicators
of buyer engagement that precede close.

Implication: Hold or Tune. A timing lag generally does not warrant a strategic change.
It may warrant a seasonal-calendar revision and a contribution-bridge update. If the
lag has compressed the latest-useful window past a tolerable threshold, Adaptation
may still be triggered.

### Classification 2 — Execution Gap

The strategy is valid but the planned activities are not happening at the required
pace, quality, or completeness. The mechanism could work; it is not being executed
sufficiently to activate it. Evidence: activity levels below plan, pipeline
progression lagging model, field coverage below required, campaign outputs below
expected.

Implication: Reinforce or Tune. If execution is the constraint, adding resources
or improving execution quality is the appropriate response. Revising the bet or
reallocating to a different bet is not.

### Classification 3 — Capacity Gap

The organization lacks the headcount, budget, systems, or partner support to execute
at planned velocity. A capacity gap often presents as an execution gap but has a
different cause and a different fix. Evidence: documented resource constraints,
budget actuals significantly below allocation, headcount shortfall, pipeline backlog
that exceeds delivery capacity.

Implication: Reinforce with resources, Resequence, or Reallocate from lower-priority
bets. If capacity cannot be added, the contribution expectation must be recalibrated.

### Classification 4 — Dependency Failure

A required internal or external dependency has not delivered on its commitment,
and the bet's execution depends on it. The bet hypothesis may be sound; the
foundation under it has shifted. Evidence: dependency status, missed dates,
substitution options, fallback plans.

Implication: Decision type depends on whether recovery is possible. If the dependency
can be recovered within the bet's remaining window, Hold with escalation. If not,
Resequence or Revise Bet depending on whether the mechanism can operate without it.

### Classification 5 — Assumption Failure

One or more load-bearing assumptions have been invalidated by evidence. The market,
buyer, product, or competitive premise behind the bet no longer holds. This is the
most consequential root cause because it means executing the strategy more completely
will not fix the problem. Evidence: structural status Invalidated, indicator readings
well below Invalidation threshold, counter-evidence confirmed.

Implication: Revise Bet or Stop. An assumption failure requires a strategic response,
not an execution response.

### Classification 6 — Measurement or Data-Quality Issue

The observed divergence reflects a data problem, attribution error, or measurement
change rather than a real-world event. The bet may be performing as expected but the
signals are not accurately captured. Evidence: data freshness issues, attribution
discrepancies, methodology changes in the underlying measurement system, known CRM
or analytics gaps.

Implication: Investigate before deciding. A measurement issue should be resolved
before any intervention is deployed. Acting on bad data produces bad interventions.

### Classification 7 — External Shock

An event outside the organization's control has materially changed the landscape
in a way that was not anticipated and could not have been planned for. A macro
event, regulatory change, competitive move, or supply disruption that changes
the ground rules for the bet.

Implication: Decision type depends on the shock's duration and reversibility. A
temporary shock may warrant Hold. A structural shift warrants Revise Bet or Revise
Commitment, with the change type recorded as External Change in the Version Ledger.

### Classification 8 — Portfolio Conflict

Resource or priority conflicts within the portfolio are degrading this bet's
performance. The bet is being starved of field attention, budget, partner capacity,
or leadership focus because competing organizational priorities are consuming what
the bet requires.

Implication: Resequence or Reallocate. Portfolio conflicts require a portfolio-level
decision, not a bet-level fix.

### Classification 9 — Upside Opportunity

Performance is above plan because a market condition, product development, or
execution element is generating more contribution than expected. The causal mechanism
should be understood before reallocation is recommended. An upside opportunity can
reflect genuine additional capacity, or it can reflect pull-forward from adjacent
bets or next-year deals.

Implication: Reinforce or Reallocate from underperforming bets — but only after
the 10-question feasibility test confirms the upside is incremental and can be
captured within the current year.

## Decision Vocabulary

Every recommendation the Loop generates is expressed in the canonical decision
vocabulary. This vocabulary distinguishes execution improvement, tactical change,
portfolio reallocation, strategic change, and formal commitment revision. Using
consistent vocabulary prevents conflating decision types and ensures that the
right approval authority is engaged.

### Hold

Continue the current course within a named observation window. The Loop will
re-evaluate at the end of that window or earlier if a trigger event occurs.

Hold is chosen when: evidence is insufficient to support a different diagnosis;
a timing lag is suspected and more signal is needed; a recently deployed intervention
is still in its window; or the cost of acting incorrectly exceeds the cost of
waiting for additional evidence.

Hold is not passive. Every Hold decision names: the specific evidence or trigger
event that would escalate to Adaptation; the latest date at which the Hold can
continue before the latest-useful window closes; and the observation activities
that will increase confidence during the window.

### Reinforce

Accelerate execution of the current bet using additional resources — budget,
headcount, partner capacity, or field attention. The bet's strategy, mechanism,
target segment, and contribution expectation do not change. Reinforcement is
chosen when execution or capacity is the binding constraint and the underlying
bet logic is supported.

### Tune

Make a tactical adjustment to the execution approach without changing the bet's
core mechanism. Tuning may involve segment prioritization, pricing adjustments,
channel mix, messaging emphasis, coverage model, or sequencing of activities.
Tuning is chosen when the mechanism is sound but specific execution elements are
underperforming.

Distinguish Tune from Revise Bet: Tuning changes how the bet is executed; revising
changes what the bet is.

### Reallocate

Shift budget, headcount, or organizational attention from one bet to another. The
source bet loses investment; the destination bet gains it. Reallocation requires
the 10-question feasibility test on the destination bet before it is recommended.
Reallocation triggers Portfolio Adaptation.

### Resequence

Change the temporal order of bets or milestones without changing which bets are
pursued or how they are ultimately resourced. Resequencing is chosen when timing
conflicts, seasonal compression, or dependency ordering requires reprioritizing
execution sequence without changing the portfolio composition.

### Revise Bet

Change the core hypothesis, target segment, or mechanism of a bet. This is a
strategic change, not a tactical one. Revising a bet produces a new bet that
replaces the prior one. The prior bet's record — hypothesis, evidence, performance
history, reason for revision — is preserved in the Version Ledger as a Revise Bet
entry. The revised bet is initialized as a new Version Ledger entry.

### Revise Commitment

Formally revise a commitment target, scope definition, or timeline. This requires
the approval authority defined for that commitment's protection level in the input
template. Revising a non-negotiable commitment requires escalation above the standard
approval chain. Every commitment revision is recorded in the Version Ledger with
its change type. Formal Target Accommodation must be clearly labeled.

### Stop

Formally discontinue a bet and release its resources to the portfolio. The bet's
record — mechanism, evidence, performance history, stop reason — is preserved.
Stopping differs from Reallocation: Stop releases resources to the portfolio rather
than directing them to a specific destination.

### Escalate

Surface a decision that requires authority, context, or stakeholders not present
in the current review.

Escalate when: a non-negotiable breach notice has been issued; a Revise Commitment
decision is required; a Reallocation will materially affect a bet or commitment
owned by a stakeholder outside the current review; or the Loop has identified a
decision that exceeds the mandate of the current audience.

An Escalate decision names: who the decision belongs to, what specific authorization
is required, what the options are, and the deadline by which the decision must be made.

## Commitment Portfolio

The Loop governs multiple simultaneous commitments. Each commitment carries its own
metric definition, inclusion and exclusion rules, target, protection level, and
revision authority. Commitments are tracked independently.

### Cross-Commitment Impact

Before surfacing any recommendation, the Loop evaluates the effect of each option
on every commitment in the portfolio and against all relevant guardrails. An option
that improves primary commitment attainability while harming a secondary commitment
or breaching a guardrail is a tradeoff, not a recommendation. The Loop surfaces
tradeoffs explicitly before any decision is made.

### Commitment Attainability States

The Loop reports attainability for each commitment independently:

**On track:** Current evidence supports reaching the commitment target within the
current fiscal year at the expected seasonal pace.

**At risk — recoverable:** Evidence indicates the commitment is behind its expected
path, but a credible recovery path exists within the current year. A recovery path
is credible when it depends on attainable actions, has a supporting contribution
bridge, and does not require assumptions whose seasonal window has already closed.

**Structurally at risk:** The commitment is behind plan and the recovery options
have narrowed materially. Recovery is possible but requires significant intervention
under favorable assumptions. The probability of reaching target without intervention
is very low.

**No longer supportable under current assumptions:** Under any credible scenario
given current evidence, the commitment is not attainable in the current fiscal year.
This is a formal Loop assessment, not a worst-case projection. It triggers a
non-negotiable breach notice if the commitment is designated non-negotiable.

**Insufficient evidence:** The Loop has not yet received enough evidence of the right
type and recency to assess attainability reliably. An evidence collection plan must
be named.

### Attainability Scenarios

When attainability is not clearly On track, the Loop provides scenarios that name
the assumptions each depends on and when the recovery window closes:

- **Base scenario:** The most likely outcome given current evidence and current
  trajectory, with the specific assumptions it depends on
- **Recovery scenario:** What would need to be true for the commitment to still be
  attainable — the interventions required, the assumptions that must hold, and the
  latest date by which those conditions must be met
- **Downside scenario:** The outcome if current negative signals continue or worsen,
  with the assumptions it depends on

### Portfolio Posture

Beyond individual commitment status, the Loop reports an overall portfolio posture.
Portfolio posture is not a weighted average of individual commitment attainability
states. It is a qualitative characterization of whether the portfolio as a whole
is positioned to meet its collective commitments, factoring in cross-commitment
conflicts, shared resource constraints, and concentration risk.

## Assumption Framework

### Two Independent Status Tracks

Every assumption carries two independent status tracks that the Loop maintains
and reports separately.

**Structural status:** Is the underlying premise still valid?
The structural track answers whether the market, product, competitive, or
organizational premise behind the assumption continues to hold. Structural status
values: Held / Weakened / Warning / Invalidated / Restored.

**Current-horizon status:** Can this assumption still generate contribution within
the current fiscal year?
The horizon track answers whether the assumption's contribution window is still
open, regardless of structural validity. A product feature that ships in August
may be structurally held — the product will ship, the market wants it — but if
the buying window required a Q2 ship date to enable Q2 close, the assumption is
current-horizon Invalidated regardless of structural status.

Current-horizon status values: Active / Deferred / Invalidated (current year) /
Restored.

These tracks are independent. The combinations matter:
- Structural Held, Horizon Active — assumption is fully operative
- Structural Held, Horizon Invalidated — contribution requires next year; assumption
  feeds next year's plan
- Structural Invalidated, Horizon Active — assumption has failed; pipeline built on
  this assumption is at risk even if mid-cycle
- Structural Restored, Horizon Invalidated — mechanism is recovering but the fiscal
  window has closed; carry-forward candidate

Once a seasonal or fiscal window has closed, current-horizon status cannot be
restored regardless of structural recovery.

### Owner-Stated vs. Evidence-Assessed Confidence

**Owner-stated confidence** is the bet owner's judgment at planning time. It is
a human assessment, not evidence.

**Evidence-assessed confidence** is the Loop's inference from the quality, quantity,
recency, and type of evidence supplied:
- Evidence type and tier
- Number of independent data points
- Recency of evidence
- Presence and direction of counter-evidence
- Whether an indicator with a defined threshold exists and has been read

Evidence-assessed confidence values: **High / Medium / Low / Insufficient evidence**

Missing evidence does not produce Low confidence. Low confidence means available
evidence actively provides weak support or meaningful contradiction. If an owner
states High confidence with no supporting evidence, the Loop returns Insufficient
evidence — unless evidence is present that actively contradicts the assumption, in
which case the Loop returns Low.

The gap between owner-stated and evidence-assessed confidence is itself a signal.
The Loop reports the gap and its likely cause. Divergence can indicate optimism,
political commitment, unsupported stretch assumptions, or missing evidence that
should be collected before the assumption can be relied upon.

### Threshold Architecture

Each assumption defines four threshold levels:

**Normal variance:** Expected noise in the indicator. Movement within this band does
not constitute a signal. This prevents the Loop from reacting to routine fluctuation.

**Warning threshold:** The indicator value or event that elevates attention. Reaching
this threshold moves the assumption to Warning status, increases monitoring frequency,
and triggers a named decision deadline. Warning alone does not trigger Adaptation.

**Invalidation threshold:** The indicator value or event that causes the Loop to
mark the assumption Invalidated. Invalidation triggers Adaptation.

**Restoration evidence:** What would need to be true to restore a Weakened or
Invalidated structural status. If restoration is structurally impossible — a
competitive window has permanently closed — this must be noted explicitly.

## Seasonality Framework

### Why Seasonality Is First-Class

Seasonal patterns determine when contribution can actually be recognized. A bet
that enters its peak window without completing pipeline-build activities has already
missed its primary window, regardless of how much activity executes during peak.
The error was made in the preparation window, not the peak window.

The Loop tracks seasonality ahead of contribution shortfalls, not after them.

### Season Position

Inferred from the bet's seasonal calendar and the current date. The user does not
state position.

Pre-season / Build / Pre-peak / Peak / Post-peak / Off-season

### Season Readiness

Inferred from readiness gate evidence and the current date relative to the peak
window. The user does not state readiness.

Not started / In progress — on track / In progress — at risk /
Ready / Missed — recoverable / Missed — unrecoverable

### Latest Useful Dates and Endgame Timing

The Loop computes per bet and per pivot option using the complete causal timing chain:

Remaining fiscal days − **intervention implementation** − **field or partner ramp**
− **pipeline or demand build** − **qualification / sales / adoption cycle** −
**procurement or operational realization** − **recognition lag**

When this value reaches zero or below, the bet or pivot option has entered the
Endgame and Learning window for that intervention type.

The **bottleneck element** is the longest stage that cannot be parallelized for
the specific intervention being evaluated. Different interventions have different
bottlenecks and therefore different Endgame thresholds. The Loop evaluates Endgame
timing separately for each pivot option during Adaptation.

The Loop reports:
- Latest useful decision date (the last date on which a decision can still affect
  this year's result for the specified intervention type)
- Latest useful deployment date (the last date a new initiative can go live and
  still generate contribution)
- The full calculation chain with each component named
- The bottleneck element and its duration
- Evidence-assessed confidence in the timing estimate
- Any missing timing inputs that reduce precision
- Any user-supplied override with recorded reason

## Readiness Gates

Seven general readiness gates apply to every bet. Gate applicability — Required /
Supporting / N/A — is set at planning time with a written justification. A gate
must not be marked N/A to avoid reporting a missed Required gate.

| Gate | What it assesses |
|------|-----------------|
| Market readiness | Is the target market ready to purchase, adopt, or engage? |
| Product readiness | Is the product or solution ready to support the bet's mechanism? |
| Field readiness | Is the go-to-market team ready to execute? |
| Partner readiness | Is the channel, distribution, or technology partner ready? |
| Pipeline or demand readiness | Is there sufficient qualified pipeline or demand signal? |
| Operational delivery readiness | Can the organization deliver at the required scale? |
| Economic readiness | Is commercial structure, pricing, and budget in place? |

The gates are general. Bet-specific evidence fills them. For a sales-led bet, Field
readiness includes champion quality, economic-buyer engagement, and competitive
displacement evidence. For a product-led bet, Market readiness includes activation
rate and retention signals. The gates do not change; the evidence that satisfies
them does.

Gate status is inferred from evidence — not from user declarations.

## External Evidence Framework

External evidence is a primary input during Planning mode and a continuing input
during Monitoring and Adaptation. The Loop synthesizes external evidence with
internal evidence to maintain accurate assumption assessments.

### Evidence Types

The Loop may receive external evidence about any of the following:
- Market growth and demand trends in the target segment
- Buyer-budget conditions and budget-cycle timing
- Competitive moves: pricing, packaging, product capability, investment, positioning,
  leadership changes, market entries or exits
- Regulatory developments affecting buyers or the organization
- Pricing and margin dynamics in the category
- Category maturity and consolidation signals
- Partner ecosystem shifts: capability, prioritization, ownership changes
- Macroeconomic conditions affecting buyer behavior, budget availability, or
  deal velocity

The Loop may consume externally supplied research, Skill-generated analysis,
competitive intelligence, or connected intelligence sources. Regardless of source,
every external signal must preserve scope and attribution.

### Attribution Requirements per External Signal

For every external signal, the Loop preserves:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Source | Name, type (analyst report, first-party research, public filing, partner conversation, field observation, etc.), and credibility tier |
| Date | When the signal was produced or observed |
| Segment | The buyer segment, market segment, or product category this signal applies to |
| Geography | The geographic scope of the signal |
| Nature | Fact / Forecast / Opinion / Anecdote |
| Reliability | The Loop's assessment of source credibility and methodology quality |
| Materiality | Which assumptions are affected and how severely if this signal reflects reality |
| Assumptions affected | The specific assumptions in the portfolio this signal is relevant to |
| Expected duration | Temporary condition / Structural shift / Unknown |
| Contradictory evidence | Other signals that point in a different direction |
| Last refresh date | When this signal was last verified or updated |

External evidence that lacks a date, source type, and scope is treated as Anecdotal
and may not be used to move an assumption to Warning or Invalidated without
corroborating evidence of a defined tier.

## Contribution Bridge

### Two Distinct Formats

**Forecast bridge:** Used from Planning through Endgame triage before fiscal close.
Expresses expected portfolio contribution adjusted for identified risks: capacity
deductions, dependency-risk adjustments, seasonal concentration adjustments,
probability adjustments, overlap deductions.

**Year-end actual reconciliation:** Used at fiscal close when actual contribution
is known. Contains only unique booked or recognized contribution attributed by bet,
with verified deductions for overlap or duplication. Forecast-risk adjustments do
not appear — they are replaced by actual results.

These formats must never be mixed. An Endgame review in October still uses the
forecast bridge. The year-end actual reconciliation is not activated by Endgame
mode — it is activated by having verified closed results.

### Year-End Actual Reconciliation

- Gross actual contribution by bet is listed first
- Verified deductions are negative amounts: confirmed overlap, metric-scope exclusions
- The result is Unique [commitment metric]
- Reducing a forecast deduction that was larger than actual overlap is not additional
  contribution. It is a smaller negative deduction.
- Forecast-risk adjustments do not appear. Once the year is closed, the risk either
  materialized or it did not.

### Evidence-Supported Base

The evidence-supported base is contribution from existing or established motions that
is not attributed to incremental strategic bets, using the formal Finance or RevOps
forecast and the same metric definition as the commitment. The evidence-supported
base is not guaranteed contribution. It may carry its own forecast risk from pipeline
timing, renewal dynamics, or partner performance. It simply does not depend on the
incremental strategic bet portfolio. The Loop does not allow the base to absorb a
bet's metric that is outside the commitment definition.

## Guardrails and Commitment Tradeoffs

### Guardrail Metrics

Every bet defines a set of guardrail metrics — floors and ceilings that must not
be violated in pursuit of primary contribution. Guardrails protect the quality,
cost, durability, and compliance of contribution, not just its volume.

Common types: CAC ceiling, gross margin floor, discount ceiling, delivery capacity
ceiling, churn or retention floor, customer or quarterly concentration ceiling,
brand or regulatory restrictions, minimum deal quality.

Guardrails are defined at bet level and checked at portfolio level. A reallocation
that improves primary commitment attainability while breaching a delivery capacity
guardrail creates a churn risk that may appear in the NRR commitment one to two
quarters later. The Loop surfaces this tradeoff before recommending the reallocation.

### Cross-Commitment and Guardrail Impact

Before recommending any intervention, the Loop evaluates and reports:
- Expected effect on primary commitment contribution
- Expected effect on each secondary commitment
- Which guardrails are at risk, under what conditions, and to what degree
- Any option that produces net negative portfolio impact when secondary commitments
  and guardrails are included

A recommendation that breaches a guardrail is surfaced as a tradeoff — not suppressed.
The decision to accept a guardrail breach belongs to the authority defined for that
guardrail's consequence in the input template.

## Pivot Comparison Framework

When Adaptation is triggered, the Loop generates an option set. No single formula
determines ranking. Options are evaluated across multiple dimensions.

### Hold Is Always Option 0

Evaluating the cost and benefit of continuing the current course always precedes
evaluating alternatives. Hold's costs: expected contribution shortfall, elapsed
intervention window, risk that later action has a shorter timing chain. Hold's
benefits: no disruption to working bets, preserved resources, optionality maintained,
avoids irreversible commitments. Both are stated before any other option.

### Replacement-Bet Feasibility

Before recommending that a bet absorb another bet's contribution gap, the Loop
evaluates ten feasibility questions:

1. Incremental addressable demand — is there unmet demand in the target segment?
2. Available pipeline — is there qualified pipeline not already attributed to this bet?
3. Additional capacity — what is required and is it available?
4. Sales-cycle feasibility — can additional volume close within the remaining window?
5. Saturation and diminishing returns — is the segment approaching saturation?
6. Cannibalization — would absorbing the gap displace other pipeline or spend?
7. Pull-forward vs. incremental — is this capturing next-year deals or genuinely new?
8. Shared dependencies — does absorbing the gap strain shared dependencies?
9. Disruption to working bets — does this reallocation harm bets that are on track?
10. Maximum credible incremental contribution — what is the realistic ceiling?

Question 10 is a ceiling, not a target. The feasibility test produces the most the
bet could plausibly deliver if all ten conditions are favorable. It is not an
expected contribution.

### Multi-Factor Comparison

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Contribution recoverable | How much of the gap can this option address? |
| Confidence in mechanism | How well-evidenced is this option's causal logic? |
| Time to first leading signal | How quickly will the Loop know if it is working? |
| Time to contribution impact | How quickly will recognized contribution change? |
| Seasonal window fit | Does this option have a viable window in the current year? |
| Timing chain | What is this option's Endgame date? |
| Resource required | Budget, headcount, partner capacity consumed |
| Opportunity cost | What does this displace or delay? |
| Reversibility | Can the organization undo this if it is wrong? |
| Disruption to working bets | Does this harm bets currently on track? |
| Effect on secondary commitments | Effect on each commitment in the portfolio |
| Guardrail risk | Which guardrails are at risk and under what conditions? |
| Strategic fit | Alignment with long-term direction |
| Downside if wrong | Cost of this option failing |
| Current-year viability | Recovery / Risk mitigation / Next-year build / Learning only |

### Current-Year Viability Classification

**Recovery:** Full original target contribution is attainable this fiscal year.

**Risk mitigation:** Reduced but meaningful contribution is attainable. Continued
investment generates real value even if the original target is not reached.

**Next-year build:** Current-year contribution is no longer attainable. Investment
positions the bet for the following year.

**Learning only:** No current-year contribution is expected. Investment generates
evidence that informs next year's plan.

## Intervention Evaluation

Every intervention the Loop recommends receives a structured evaluation record.
The Loop does not merely note that an intervention was deployed — it evaluates
whether the intervention changed the outcome and recommends a follow-on decision.

### Evaluation Record Fields

**Baseline:** The measured state of the key indicator(s) immediately before the
intervention was deployed. The baseline is the comparison point for all subsequent
signal readings.

**Intended mechanism:** The causal chain this intervention is expected to activate.
How is this intervention expected to change the key indicator, and why? A mechanism
that cannot be stated cannot be evaluated.

**Expected leading signal:** The upstream indicator that should change before
contribution changes. The first thing the Loop expects to see move if the
intervention is working. Named before the intervention is deployed.

**Expected magnitude or range:** The quantitative change expected in the leading
signal and the ultimate contribution indicator. Stated as a range with a rationale,
not as a point estimate.

**Signal date:** The date by which the leading signal should be visible. If the
signal is not observed by this date, that is itself evidence about the intervention.

**Observation window:** The period over which the Loop observes before concluding.
The window must be long enough to distinguish signal from noise and short enough
to allow follow-on decisions within the remaining fiscal window.

**Comparison method:** How the Loop will assess the result — against the baseline,
against the prior period, against the seasonally expected path, against a reference
group, or against a defined target.

**Confounders:** Other factors changing simultaneously that could explain any
observed result independent of the intervention. Named before the window closes
so they are not invented after results are known.

**Guardrail impact:** Which guardrails are affected by this intervention, in which
direction, and with what observed result.

### Intervention Results

**Effective:** The leading signal moved as expected within the window and the
contribution impact is consistent with the stated mechanism.

**Partially effective:** The leading signal moved but less than expected, or some
mechanism components worked and others did not. A specific statement of what worked
and what did not is required.

**Ineffective:** The leading signal did not move, moved in the wrong direction, or
contribution impact was not observed. The mechanism did not activate as expected.

**Inconclusive:** The observation window elapsed but confounders, data-quality issues,
or insufficient evidence prevent a clear assessment. An inconclusive result is not
a neutral result — the Loop names what evidence would have been required for a
conclusive assessment and whether it can still be collected.

### Follow-On Decision

Every intervention result generates a follow-on decision from the canonical vocabulary:
- Effective → Hold or Reinforce (the mechanism is working; evaluate whether to
  continue or accelerate)
- Partially effective → Tune (refine the elements that did not work while preserving
  those that did)
- Ineffective → Revise Bet or Stop (the mechanism did not activate; a strategic
  response is likely required)
- Inconclusive → Hold with evidence-collection plan, or Escalate if the Endgame
  timing chain can no longer support another observation cycle

## Non-Negotiable Breach Protocol

When evidence indicates that a commitment designated non-negotiable has become
unattainable under any credible scenario, the Loop issues a formal breach notice.

The breach notice contains:
- Which commitment is at risk
- The specific evidence that indicates non-attainability
- The set of scenarios under which attainability could be restored, if any exist
- The decision required from leadership, and the authority it requires
- The deadline by which the decision must be made

The Loop does not decide what to do with a non-negotiable breach. It does not
silently route the commitment to Endgame. It does not revise the target downward.
It surfaces the breach and the decision it requires.

A breach notice is distinct from a standard Adaptation option set. Standard
Adaptation produces options. A breach notice produces a decision escalation. They
may coexist — the Loop may issue a breach notice and generate an option set
simultaneously — but the breach notice always precedes and frames the options.

## Version Ledger and Plan Immutability

### The Governing Rule

The Loop may revise the plan. It must never rewrite what the organization originally
believed.

This applies to: commitment targets, assumption statements, warning and invalidation
thresholds, seasonal windows, latest useful dates, expected contribution by period,
metric definitions, inclusion and exclusion rules, and intervention success criteria.

When any of these values changes, the original value is preserved alongside the
revised value, date, evidence, decision owner, approver, change type, bridge impact,
and prior assessments calculated under the old value.

### Change Types

**New learning:** Evidence has arrived that genuinely changes what the organization
knows. Market behavior, competitive moves, product delivery, or buyer response
differs from what was assumed.

**External change:** An event outside the organization's control has changed the
facts the assumption was based on.

**Execution correction:** The original value contained a factual error now identified.

**Formal target accommodation:** The target or threshold is being revised to match
changed expectations rather than changed evidence. The organization is resetting
what it expects, not recording what it has learned.

Formal target accommodation must be labeled clearly and preserved permanently. A plan
revised repeatedly through formal target accommodation is a fundamentally different
plan than one that achieved its original commitment. The Loop preserves this
distinction across every revision. It is the most consequential change type because
it can be used to make a struggling plan appear healthy.

### Prior Assessments

When a value is revised, prior assessments calculated under the old value are
retained. If a Warning was issued under the old threshold and a new threshold would
not have triggered Warning, the Warning assessment remains in the record. It cannot
be retroactively replaced.

## Three Separate Record Types

The Loop maintains three distinct record types. They are not interchangeable. Each
records a different class of event and serves a different organizational function.

### Record Type 1 — Version Ledger

**What it captures:** Revisions to plan values.

The Version Ledger records any change to a value that was part of the approved plan:
commitment targets, assumption statements, warning and invalidation thresholds,
seasonal windows, expected contribution by period, metric definitions, inclusion and
exclusion rules, and intervention success criteria.

The Version Ledger does not record operational events, status inferences, or
intervention outcomes. Those belong in the other two record types below.

Each Version Ledger entry contains: revision ID and date, item revised, original
value, revised value, change type (New learning / External change / Execution
correction / Formal target accommodation), evidence that prompted the revision,
decision owner, approver, bridge impact, and prior assessments calculated under
the old value.

### Record Type 2 — State Transition and Assessment History

**What it captures:** Loop-inferred status changes.

The State Transition and Assessment History records every instance where the Loop
infers a change in status across any of the six state dimensions — operating mode,
bet maturity, operational status, assumption status (structural or current-horizon),
season position, or season readiness.

This record exists to distinguish Loop-inferred status changes from plan value
changes (Version Ledger) and from intervention outcomes (Intervention Evaluation
Record). It provides an auditable chain of when the Loop first assessed a condition,
under what evidence, and what status it assigned.

Entries include: transition ID and date, state dimension, prior status, new status,
evidence that triggered the transition, confidence level, and any decision deadline
set as a result.

This record is where entries like "Assumption A2 maintained at Held — current
indicator reading within Warning threshold" belong. They record an assessment
outcome, not a plan value revision.

### Record Type 3 — Intervention Evaluation Record

**What it captures:** Intervention lifecycle from deployment through result assessment.

The Intervention Evaluation Record documents each intervention from the moment it
is deployed through its formal result assessment. It captures whether the
intervention changed the outcome, what mechanism was affected, what evidence
supports the result, and what the follow-on decision is.

This record is separate from the Version Ledger because an intervention result is
not a plan revision — it is a learning event. If an intervention generates a plan
revision as a consequence (e.g., a bet hypothesis is revised based on what the
intervention revealed), that revision then receives a Version Ledger entry. The
intervention result itself stays in the Intervention Evaluation Record.

Each entry contains: intervention ID, bet, date deployed, root-cause classification
addressed, description of the intervention, baseline reading, expected leading
signal and signal date, expected magnitude, observation window, observed readings
with dates, confounder analysis, formal result (Effective / Partially effective /
Ineffective / Inconclusive), follow-on decision type and rationale.

### Why These Records Are Separate

Mixing these record types produces an unreliable audit trail. The most consequential
error is recording an assumption status assessment as a Version Ledger entry — this
conflates an observation with a plan revision, which makes the plan appear to have
been formally changed when it was only being monitored. Similarly, recording an
intervention outcome as a Version Ledger entry implies the plan was revised based
on the intervention when it may not have been.

Keep these records separate. Record plan value changes in the Version Ledger. Record
Loop status inferences in the State Transition History. Record intervention outcomes
in the Intervention Evaluation Record.

## Relationship to Other Loops

The Annual GTM Strategy and Goal Governance Loop sits at the top of the GTM Loop
hierarchy. It consumes outputs from subordinate Loops as evidence to inform
assumption status and health assessments.

### Win/Loss Pattern Loop

Win/Loss findings affect assumptions in the Annual GTM Loop — particularly
assumptions about champion profile, competitive dynamics, product-market fit, and
segment addressability. Imported Win/Loss findings require source-specific attribution:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Mechanism confidence | How confident is the evidence that the named factor caused the win or loss? |
| Prevalence confidence | How reliably does this factor apply across the relevant segment, not only the deals sampled? |
| Cohort | Which deal set was analyzed? |
| Period | When were these deals conducted and sourced? |
| Denominator | How many deals does this finding cover? |
| Comparison context | Win-rate change, absolute count, or qualitative pattern? |
| Scope limitations | Which segments, geographies, deal sizes, or use cases are included or excluded? |
| Last validation date | When was this finding last confirmed against new evidence? |
| Assumptions affected | Which specific Annual GTM Loop assumptions does this finding affect? |

### Pipeline Risk Loop

Pipeline Risk findings affect Annual GTM Loop assumptions about pipeline coverage,
stage conversion, deal velocity, and the reliability of pipeline-based contribution
forecasts. Imported Pipeline Risk findings require their own source-specific schema:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Portfolio scope | Which pipeline is included? Stage range, segment, geography, date range |
| As-of date | The date to which this pipeline snapshot applies |
| Risk definition | What constitutes a "risk" in this finding? |
| Stage or cohort | Which portion of the pipeline does this finding cover? |
| Denominator | Total pipeline from which the risk is measured |
| Risk concentration | Whether risk is concentrated in specific segments, stages, or time buckets |
| Timing | When the at-risk pipeline is expected to close vs. the commitment's required timing |

| Current status | Has the risk materialized, is it active, or has it been resolved? |
| Assumptions affected | Which specific Annual GTM Loop assumptions does this finding affect? |

The Annual GTM Loop does not replace these Loops. It consumes their outputs as
attributed evidence. Attribution cannot be combined or collapsed: findings from
different Loops may point to the same assumption, but they are preserved as
separate evidence items with separate confidence assessments.

## What This Loop Does Not Do

**It does not invent strategy from no evidence.** During Planning mode, the Loop
synthesizes what is known into a testable portfolio. It challenges unsupported
assumptions and flags risk concentrations. It does not fill gaps in evidence with
optimism or in leadership intent with invention.

**It does not declare operating mode.** The user supplies review context. The Loop
infers mode from evidence. A user reporting an annual planning review when evidence
supports Monitoring receives a Monitoring output that also addresses the planning
context. Mode inference cannot be suppressed.

**It does not accept status inputs from users.** Users supply evidence. The Loop
infers all status dimensions — assumption status, bet maturity, health levels,
root cause, seasonal position, readiness, dependency confidence.

**It does not treat owner-stated confidence as evidence.** High confidence from the
bet owner with no supporting evidence produces Insufficient evidence from the Loop —
not High, not Medium, not Low.

**It does not conflate missing evidence with contradictory evidence.** An assumption
with no evidence receives Insufficient evidence. An assumption with evidence that
actively challenges it receives Low. These are different findings with different
implications.

**It does not recommend interventions without surfacing guardrail tradeoffs.** Every
recommendation is checked against guardrail thresholds before it is surfaced. Breaches
are reported, not hidden. The decision to accept a breach belongs to the authority
defined for that guardrail.

**It does not decide what to do with a non-negotiable breach.** It issues the notice
and names the decision required. Leadership decides.

**It does not rewrite original plan values.** A revised assumption is preserved
alongside its original. A revised target is preserved alongside its original.
Formal target accommodation is labeled as such.

**It does not mark an intervention closed without evaluating its result.** An
intervention deployed without a follow-on evaluation is an incomplete cycle. The
Loop does not consider an Adaptation cycle closed until the Intervention Evaluation
record has a result classification and a follow-on decision.

## When to Use This Loop

Use this Loop when:

- Forming and pressure-testing the annual GTM portfolio at the start of the fiscal
  year, using leadership intent, evidence, capacity, and prior-year learning
- Running a monthly operating review against the initialized plan
- Approaching a QBR and needing to know whether the plan is still supportable
  before the meeting, not after
- A load-bearing assumption has crossed a Warning threshold and a named decision
  deadline is required
- A dependency has missed its date and the portfolio implications must be assessed
- A bet is significantly outperforming and the organization must decide whether
  to accelerate investment
- A non-negotiable commitment is in question and a structured breach assessment
  is required
- An intervention has completed its observation window and a result must be assessed
- Closing the fiscal year and capturing carry-forward learning for next year's plan

The Loop requires a plan to govern. Planning mode produces that plan. No other step
precedes it — but Planning mode must have leadership intent, evidence, constraints,
and capacity inputs before it can produce a credible portfolio. The Loop does not
generate a portfolio from nothing.
